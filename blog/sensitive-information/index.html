Sensitive information, in ordinary language

People use the phrase for health data, for secrets, and for things they would not say out loud.

2 Oct 2026 · Vipra

“Sensitive” is doing several jobs in normal speech. Sometimes it means a special kind of personal data: health, biometrics, beliefs, sex life. Sometimes it means a secret: a password, a card number, an unreleased number. Sometimes it just means “I would lower my voice.”

All three are reasons to pause. They are not the same test.

A prompt can be sensitive with no identifier

“We’re about to let two people go in the Berlin office and I need a kinder script” may contain no email address. It is still a personnel matter. A tool that searches for emails will not save you from that sentence. Removing the city and the headcount, or not asking in a personal chat at all, is the actual decision.

And a secret can be sensitive without being about a person

An API key, a login link with a token in it, or a card number is sensitive because someone can use it. Vipra does look for common shapes of those, along with everyday personal details, and it can swap a finding for a token before you send. It still will not understand a paragraph that is sensitive only because of what it means.

If you would not want it forwarded, do not treat a clean review panel as permission. “You’re clear” means none of the known types are left in the field. It does not mean the thought is public.
